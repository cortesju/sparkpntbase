Community bases are somebody's own base station, a farmer's or a surveyor's or a hobbyist's, shared publicly through the RTK2go caster. You can point a rover at one and get a live fix, which is why they are here. Free, no guarantee: nobody is on the hook if one disappears tomorrow, and coverage is wherever people happen to have put one.

State network stations are the antennas operated by the agency that runs each state's network, for the states where we could identify that agency. NGS publishes their positions as part of the CORS metadata, so these are the network's real physical footprint rather than an estimate. Two honest caveats: a station being registered with NGS does not prove it is in the network's live solution on any given day, and with a VRS mountpoint you never connect to one specific station anyway, the correction is computed for where you are standing. What this layer is genuinely good for is seeing how dense the coverage is around the place your customer actually works, and how far the nearest antenna is.

We tried to read station positions from the casters themselves first. Of 16 probed, 5 answered without credentials and every one reported 0,0, which is correct behaviour for a VRS mountpoint rather than a fault. That is why this comes from the NGS metadata instead.

CORS stations used to be on this map and have been taken off. They are permanent government reference stations that feed OPUS post-processing, and you cannot connect to one for a live fix, so on a map about real-time corrections they implied something they do not offer. They belong with post-processing.

    Why corrections at all

    Standalone GNSS lands within a meter or a few: atmosphere, clocks, orbits, multipath. RTK cancels those errors against a base and resolves carrier phase to ~1 cm.

    OSR vs SSR

    OSR is a correction made for where you're standing (network RTK, 1–2 cm, needs nearby stations). SSR is a weather map of GNSS errors broadcast over a continent (Flex 3–6 cm, HAS ~20 cm) - no gaps, slightly coarser.

    Fix vs Float

    Float: corrections arriving, the receiver still solving the last math step - decimeter. Fix: solved - centimeter, the green dot. Stuck in Float = sky view, distance, or correction format.

    The ~10 km rule

    Base and rover must share the same slice of atmosphere. Past ~10 km performance degrades; past ~35 km, Float is expected behavior.

    CORS ≠ a live service

    CORS is the national skeleton of permanent stations. It mainly feeds post-processing (OPUS). Real-time networks are separate services layered on the same idea.

    Precision ≠ accuracy

    A base on a guessed (Survey-In) position gives beautifully precise measurements of the wrong place. Permanent installs always use Fixed Base with true coordinates.
