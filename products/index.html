What stands behind the hardware

Warranty, real engineering help, and the one device in the family built to be upgraded.

One-year warranty

Every unit carries a one-year warranty, and on the Facet FP, swapping the GNSS engine module yourself does not void it. Older generations (Facet, Express, Surveyor, Torch) are still supported for repairs.

Talk to the people who built it

The firmware is open source, the schematics are published, and the docs and community forum are public. When a problem needs engineering, support brings in the engineers who wrote the firmware - your question does not stop at a ticket wall.

The FP grows with you

The Facet FP is the upgradeable one: its GNSS engine is a module you swap with a screwdriver. Start with the FPL engine and move up to FPX or FPM later, or into a future generation, without buying a new device. Try it below.

Good to know before you call

The six facts that solve most support conversations before they start.

Correction format matters

The TX2 and FPL accept modern MSM correction messages (MSM3 to MSM7, per the Quectel LG290P protocol spec). Older legacy message types (RTCM 1001-1012) are not supported and quietly do nothing. If a unit will not lock on a source, check the message format first; a factory reset clears leftover settings.

Two firmware generations

Older units run per-model firmware with differing menus. Everything current runs RTK Everywhere: one codebase, the same menus on every product, any correction source. First question on any call: "which firmware are you on?"

Two connection paths

Corrections can come through your phone app or through the device's own WiFi - run one, not both. The device's corrections-sources screen always shows who is really delivering.

The L-Band change

The North American satellite-delivered service ended December 31, 2025. Facet L-Band units still work fully as receivers - they now use internet corrections instead. A global replacement is announced without a date, so we do not promise one.

Warranty & repairs

One year, and swapping FP engine modules does not void it. Zero Downtime Protection ($600-$1,000) ships a replacement before the repair even starts.

The timing line

The SXT and SXT-D are precision clocks, not mapping tools - a different vocabulary (pulse-per-second, holdover). Our engineering team handles these conversations directly.

Older products: Facet, Facet L-Band, Express, Surveyor, and Torch are all still supported for repairs. Facet L-Band owners: your unit works fully with internet corrections (see "The L-Band change" above). Surveyor and Express predate our Apple certification, so iPhone serial connections are honestly not supported on those two generations.

How we compare

There are excellent instruments across this industry. Here is where each name is strong, and where we choose to be different: open firmware, repairable hardware, and honest total cost.
Our view, simplified: positions reflect public pricing and published repair policies, not performance. Once any of these has an RTK lock, the physics is the same - about 1 cm.

Trimble

Mature, deeply featured, tightly integrated with their own corrections (RTX, global SSR). Integrated systems commonly run $10,000–$20,000+, with subscriptions stacking on top. Their premium buys robustness at the edges (canopy, fix speed in ugly conditions, ecosystem) - not a smaller number. Same physics: an RTK fix is ~1 cm regardless of logo.

Topcon

Same tier as Trimble, own corrections network (Topnet Live). Note: the "PointPerfect Live is a Topcon resell" theory is unverified - do not repeat to customers.

Emlid

Affordable, approachable, unified firmware across the Reach line. But: Reach RX is rover-only (TX2 does base + rover), and their base-capable units cost enough to erase the price advantage. Differentiators vs Emlid: open-source firmware you can audit, right-to-repair with published schematics, swappable engine on FP, MFi, NGS-calibrated antennas.

u-blox, Septentrio, Quectel - our suppliers, not rivals

They make the engines inside everyone's gear, including ours. The pitch: the same premium engines in approachable, repairable, open-firmware units at a fraction of Trimble money.

Comparing us to a premium quote?

Same physics

An RTK fix is ~1 cm regardless of the logo. If the job doesn't need the edge-case robustness, the premium buys nothing.

Total cost

Features and corrections locked behind subscriptions widen the gap over time. Ours don't.

Versatility

Our units switch roles - base today, rover tomorrow. Their gear tends to be locked into one role per device.

Repairability

Open firmware, published schematics, and on the Facet FP a swappable GNSS engine. A failed part is a repair, not a service contract.

Our first question is always: "what accuracy do you actually need?" Often the premium quote is more than the job requires - and sometimes even our own base-rover pair is, and a single TX2 with affordable corrections does it. We would rather sell you the smaller right answer.
